Universal design for the next generation

The web is for everyone. Design like it.

Great design isn't just how a site looks. It is whether every person can use it: on a phone in the sun, with a screen reader, with one hand, or with no mouse at all. This site shows you what universal design is, why it matters, and how to build it.

Disability isn't rare. It's a spectrum.

Some disabilities last a lifetime, some last a few weeks, and some last only as long as the situation you are in. Design for one end of the spectrum and you help everyone along it.

  • Permanent

    Someone who is blind uses a screen reader every day to read, shop, and study online.

  • Temporary

    Someone with a broken wrist has to browse with one hand or only a keyboard for a few weeks.

  • Situational

    Someone watching a video on a loud train relies on captions, just like someone who is deaf.

Five things you can do today

  1. Use real HTML: headings in order, lists, buttons, and landmarks like <nav> and <main>.
  2. Give every meaningful image alt text that says what it shows.
  3. Check your text contrast: at least 4.5 to 1 for normal text.
  4. Unplug your mouse and make sure you can reach everything with the Tab key.
  5. Connect every form field to a visible label.
