The numbers

About 1.3 billion people, roughly 1 in 6 worldwide, live with a significant disability, according to the World Health Organization.

  • How many people a site leaves out when it isn't accessible
  • Everyone ages, and almost everyone has a temporary or situational disability at some point

The curb cut effect

Curb cuts were made for wheelchair users, but they help everyone pushing a stroller, riding a bike, or rolling a suitcase.

  • Captions in a loud room
  • Dark mode and adjustable text size
  • Keyboard shortcuts and clear writing

It is the law

In many places, accessible websites are a legal requirement.

  • The Americans with Disabilities Act and Section 508 in the U.S.
  • The European Accessibility Act, in effect since June 2025
  • Website accessibility lawsuits

It is good business and good design

Accessible sites reach more people and tend to be better built.

  • A bigger audience and more customers
  • Overlap with search engine optimization, mobile usability, and speed
  • Cheaper to build in from the start than to fix later

It is the right thing to do

The web was meant to be universal. Leaving people out of information, school, jobs, and services is a real harm.

  • Access for everyone as part of the web's original vision
  • Real stories of people locked out of sites

This is our generation's move

The web has changed through grassroots pushes before: CSS Zen Garden for design, web standards for code, and HTML5 semantics for content. Universal design is the next one.

  • Designers our age making accessibility the default
  • A call to action for your next project
