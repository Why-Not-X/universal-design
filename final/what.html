Universal design means making things that work for as many people as possible, without anyone needing a special version. On the web, it is the difference between a site some people can use and a site everyone can use.

What universal design means

The term comes from architect Ron Mace and the Center for Universal Design at North Carolina State University, who described seven principles for designing for everyone.

  • Equitable use
  • Flexibility in use
  • Simple and intuitive
  • Perceptible information
  • Tolerance for error
  • Low physical effort
  • Size and space for approach and use

Universal design on the web

Accessibility, usability, and inclusive design overlap. Accessibility makes sure people with disabilities can use a site; universal design aims for a site that works well for everyone from the start.

  • Accessibility vs. usability vs. inclusive design
  • The web's built-in promise: content that works on any device, for any person

Who it is for

Disability can be permanent, temporary, or situational, and it covers far more than vision.

  • Visual, auditory, motor, cognitive, and speech disabilities
  • Permanent, temporary, and situational examples
  • Aging users, slow connections, small screens, and bright sunlight

How people actually use the web

Many people browse with tools that change how a page is read or controlled.

  • Screen readers like VoiceOver, NVDA, and JAWS
  • Keyboard-only and switch navigation
  • Screen magnifiers, zoom, and high contrast modes
  • Captions, transcripts, and voice control

The rules of the road

The W3C's Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) turn universal design into testable rules.

  • The W3C and the Web Accessibility Initiative
  • The four POUR principles: Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, Robust
  • Conformance levels A, AA, and AAA

Myths vs. facts

A few common excuses for skipping accessibility, and why they don't hold up.

  • “Accessible sites are ugly”
  • “Only a few people need it”
  • “It's too expensive”
  • “We'll add it at the end”
