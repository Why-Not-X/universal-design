sports_motorsports Motorsport covers far more than cars going around a track. Some series race single-seat cars at over 200 mph, some send trucks across deserts for two weeks, and some time a single car up a mountain road. The table below compares fourteen of the biggest, then the survey asks about your own favorite car.

Motorsports at a glance

table_chart Each series is listed with its abbreviation, the kind of racing, the vehicles used, its first season, where it is most popular, and the year that stands out most in its history.

menu_book What do the racing types mean?
Open-wheel racing
Single-seat cars whose wheels sit outside the bodywork, like Formula One and IndyCar.
Rally
Cars race one at a time against the clock on closed public roads, often gravel, snow, or dirt.
Rallycross
Short, wheel-to-wheel races on tracks that mix pavement and dirt.
Rally raid
Long-distance off-road racing across deserts and other rough terrain, lasting up to two weeks.
Endurance racing
Teams of drivers share one car in races lasting up to 24 hours.
Drag racing
Two vehicles race side by side in a straight line, usually over a quarter mile or less.
Hill climb
Each driver races alone up a mountain road, and the fastest time wins.
